# Photo Brief #2 — All Other Site Images

**For:** ТОРИН КОМПЮТЪРС — torin.bg redesign
**Created:** 2026-08-05 (Phase 2)
**Status:** Awaiting owner-supplied photography

---

## Why this exists

This covers every image slot in the redesign **other than** the six service-category images (those are in Photo Brief #1). None of these block launch — the design works without them — but each one makes the site measurably more credible.

They're listed in priority order. If only a few ever get taken, take the ones at the top.

**General shooting guidance is the same as Photo Brief #1** — landscape, phone main lens, bright even light, no flash, full resolution, clean background, no customer data visible.

---

## Priority 1 — highest impact

### 1.1 · The shop front / entrance
**Where it's used:** Contact section, footer, Google Business Profile, About page
**What it should show:** The building entrance as a customer approaches it, with the sign visible.

**Why it matters most:** People bringing in an expensive laptop want to know the place is real and to recognise it when they arrive. A shopfront photo answers "is this a legitimate business?" faster than any amount of copy.

**Recommended:** Straight-on, daylight, from roughly where someone would stand on the pavement. Include enough surroundings to make it recognisable — the door, the sign, the street context.

**Avoid:** Night shots, extreme angles, cars blocking the view.

---

### 1.2 · The workbench / workshop, wide
**Where it's used:** Homepage trust section, About page, possibly the hero
**What it should show:** The working environment as a whole — benches, tools, equipment, organised and in use.

**Why it matters:** Proves there's a real technical operation behind the site, not a middleman who sends work elsewhere. Visible specialist equipment — microscope, hot-air station, oscilloscope — signals capability at a glance.

**Recommended:** Wide shot from a corner, taking in several workstations. Tidy but not sterile — a working bench should look worked-at. Turn on the task lighting.

**Avoid:** Empty rooms, clutter, anything that looks like a storage cupboard.

---

### 1.3 · Microscope / board-level station
**Where it's used:** DIFF-03 (BGA expertise), category 4, About page
**What it should show:** The soldering microscope or rework station, ideally with a board under it.

**Why it matters:** This is your strongest differentiator made visible. Most competitors only swap modules; equipment like this proves you repair at component level. Worth photographing well.

**Recommended:** Close enough that the equipment is clearly identifiable, with a motherboard positioned under it. Hands and a soldering iron in frame make it stronger still.

---

## Priority 2 — strong additions

### 2.1 · Technician at work (portrait-ish)
**Where it's used:** About page, trust section
**What it should show:** A real technician working — concentrated on a repair, not posing.

**Why it matters:** Puts a human being behind the service. Repair is a trust purchase; a face makes it personal.

**Requires consent** from whoever is photographed. If nobody's comfortable being identifiable, shoot over the shoulder or focus on hands at work — nearly as effective.

**Avoid:** Stiff posed portraits, forced smiles at the camera, crossed arms.

---

### 2.2 · Hands working on a device, close
**Where it's used:** Hero (if a photo variant is ever wanted), section breaks, category pages
**What it should show:** Tight crop — hands, tools, device. No face needed.

**Why it matters:** The most versatile image type. Works at any size, always reads as "real work happening," and carries no privacy concerns.

**Recommended:** Take several of these whenever convenient. They're useful everywhere and you can never have too many.

---

### 2.3 · Diagnostics in progress
**Where it's used:** «Безплатна диагностика» trust block, self-test tool feature
**What it should show:** A device connected to test equipment, or a diagnostic screen mid-run.

**Why it matters:** Free diagnostics is one of your strongest conversion hooks and currently has nothing visual attached to it.

**Avoid:** Screens showing customer data — use a BIOS screen, a boot screen, or a hardware test.

---

### 2.4 · Spare parts / component stock
**Where it's used:** `rezervni-chasti.html`, the «Лаптопи и части» nav section
**What it should show:** Organised component stock — screens, keyboards, batteries, fans in labelled storage.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates you hold parts, implying faster turnaround than a shop that orders everything in.

---

### 2.5 · Used laptops available
**Where it's used:** `laptopi.html`
**What it should show:** Several refurbished laptops presented cleanly, ready for sale.

**Why it matters:** The page currently says "обадете се за наличности" with nothing to look at. Even a few honest photos would make it a real page.

**Note:** only worth shooting if this line is still active — see OWNER-QUESTIONS #15.

## Priority 3 — nice to have

### 3.1 · Tools and equipment detail
Close-ups of specialist tooling — reballing stencils, hot-air station, ultrasonic cleaner, oscilloscope. Useful as texture and section breaks, and quietly reinforces expertise.

### 3.2 · Battery regeneration process
Specifically the Panasonic-cell regeneration work — opening a pack, the cells themselves, the regeneration setup. Currently a genuine differentiator with no imagery. *(Placement is under review — see OWNER-QUESTIONS #9.)*

### 3.3 · Team photo
The whole team together, if there's more than one person. Humanises the About page.

### 3.4 · Certificates, awards, brand authorisations
Anything hanging on the wall worth showing. Photograph flat-on, well-lit, no glare from glass.

---

## Not needed

- **Hero background** — the design uses a brand-colour gradient by choice, not for lack of a photo. Zero dependency here.
- **Category icons** — covered by Photo Brief #1.
- **Stock photography of any kind** — actively harmful. A generic stock photo of a smiling person with a laptop undercuts every real photo next to it. If a real photo doesn't exist, the icon or gradient stays.

---

## Sending them over

Originals only — no screenshots, no messenger-compressed copies, no resizing. WeTransfer, Google Drive or similar. Batch them whenever convenient; there's no deadline, and the site improves incrementally as each arrives.
