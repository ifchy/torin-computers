<objective>
Close the two CSS cascade defects that stop Phase 2's success criterion 1 from being true.
Both are specificity collisions in hand-authored stylesheets with no build step, both were
independently reproduced from the cascade by the verifier, and both live in files whose comments
assert the passing ratios the cascade then overrides — which is exactly why a documentation-level
review missed them.

**CR-01** — the hero's prose colour rule matches the trust badge as a bare paragraph and wins on
specificity, so «Безплатна диагностика» — the D-15 above-the-fold conversion element — ships at
1.06:1 in Theme B and 1.29:1 in Theme A. Effectively invisible, on the homepage, above the fold,
in both themes. The same collision also overrides the badge's own top margin and so invalidates
the hero-height arithmetic recorded in the file.

**CR-02** — `outline-offset: 2px` draws the focus ring entirely on the surface *behind* the
button, never on the amber fill the comment reasons about. The amber-button ring rule and the
dark-surface ring rule tie at equal specificity, so the later one wins everywhere and the six
most important controls on the site give keyboard users a ring at 1.03–1.21:1.

Purpose: "displays correctly on mobile and desktop viewports" is the literal wording of success
criterion 1. Neither of these is cosmetic polish — one hides a conversion element, the other
removes the visible focus indicator from every primary call-to-action on a dark surface. Both
have their correct tokens already declared and unused.

Output: two corrected stylesheets, deployed and re-fetched from the live staging origin, with
every ratio recomputed rather than inherited from the review — which understated two of them.

**Scope fence.** This plan touches `src/css/components.css` and `src/css/base.css` and nothing
else. CR-03 through CR-06 and WR-01 through WR-17 (other than WR-08 and WR-10, owned by plans
02-06 and 02-07) are deliberately out of scope for this pass.
</objective>

Two edits, both in the focus block of base.css. The mechanism is that a ring drawn with
`outline-offset: 2px` never touches the button's own fill — it is painted on the surface behind
the button, in the 2px gap and outward — so the ring colour must be chosen against the *surface*,
never against the button.

**1. Add the call bar to the dark-surface ring group.** The rule at base.css:163-164 currently
lists `.hero :focus-visible` and `.site-footer :focus-visible`. Add `.callbar :focus-visible` as
a third selector in the same group, at the same (0,2,0). `.callbar` is inside neither of the other
two — it is a `position: fixed` element that is a sibling of `<main>`, not a descendant of the
hero or the footer — and that omission is precisely the trap that produced this defect. Adding it
here means every dark surface on the site declares its on-dark ring once, in one place.

**2. Add a (0,3,0) group that beats the amber-button rule on dark surfaces.** Keep
`.btn--primary:focus-visible { outline-color: var(--c-on-brand); }` at base.css:168 exactly as it
is — it is correct for the three category pages, where the button sits on white and #16223a
measures 15.86:1. Immediately after it, add a new rule whose selector list is
`.hero .btn--primary:focus-visible`, `.site-footer .btn--primary:focus-visible` and

dark-surface value wins by specificity rather than by being written later, which is what makes the
fix survive a future reordering. Do not use an importance flag and do not add an id selector.

**3. Correct the comment at base.css:166-167.** It currently reasons "On amber the ring is the
dark on-brand ink (10.14:1) — the lighter amber ring is invisible there", which is false in two
ways: the ring is never drawn on the amber, and the rule as written wins on dark surfaces too.
Rewrite it to state (a) the mechanism — a 2px outline offset paints the ring on the surface behind
the button, so the ring is measured against that surface and never against the button fill; (b) the
